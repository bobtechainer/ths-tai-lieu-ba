## Slide 3 — Where things stand today

*A fragmented experience, with information scattered across systems*

- Time lost, productivity down
- A fragmented experience
- Reports arrive late and rarely answer what was asked
- Digital transformation falls short of expectations

**1. In the classroom** — Hardware runs on its own, disconnected from the school's management system. Teachers set everything up by hand at the start of each period, which eats into class time and invites mistakes.

**2. At school level** — Teachers prepare lessons and enter marks manually. Learning data sits in separate places, so no one can pull a report quickly when the authorities ask for one.

**3. At department and ministry level** — There is no continuous data flow from the classroom up to the managing agencies. Reports come late, and there is little real data to judge how digital transformation is actually going.

## Slide 5 — An ecosystem for interactive teaching

*AI · Data · Interaction · Personalisation · Assessment*

**The old lesson cycle**

1. Lesson prep — PowerPoint, photocopies, hunting for scattered materials
2. Class setup — Roll call by voice or on paper (3–5 minutes)
3. Delivery — Blackboard and slides, one way only
4. Student interaction — Hands up, spoken answers (only 2–3 students per period)
5. Assessment — Marked by hand, results days later
6. Reporting — Paper mark books, no trend analysis

**Inputs**

- Teachers — Lesson prep, content, learning materials and activities
- Students — Take part, interact, give feedback
- Classroom equipment — AI camera, display, microphone, personal devices
- Digital content — Video, slides, quizzes, games, reference materials

**At the centre: digital resource and smart interaction platform (AI – Data – Cloud)**

- Digital resource library
- AI camera recognition
- Varied interaction
- Real-time assessment
- AI dashboards and reports
- Security and privacy

**Core toolset**

- Interactive whiteboard + 3D widgets + video
- AI attendance camera (2 seconds, fully hands-off)
- Real-time quizzes, polls, game mode, chat — the whole class takes part
- Automatic marking, running results, personalised feedback
- AI dashboard: objectives not yet met, students who need support

**Outcomes**

- Higher interaction — every student takes part; focus and interest go up
- Deeper understanding — instant feedback, personalised learning
- Fairer assessment — automatic, accurate marking that saves time
- Decisions based on data — trend analysis to improve teaching and learning

**What the school gains**

- Better teaching quality
- Less time spent on operations
- Standardised processes
- Stronger reputation and brand

**Longer term**

- Students develop across the board
- Teachers are more creative and more effective
- The school's digital transformation is built to last
- A society where learning runs on smart schools

**Ecosystem and partners**

- Schools — Manage and run the system, set the direction for digital transformation and quality
- Parents — Follow their child's progress, stay in step with the school
- Content partners — Quality learning materials, standardised, kept up to date and aligned with the curriculum

## Slide 7 — What a Smart School makes possible

**Operations**

- Full digital transformation: replace scattered teaching practice with a central LMS/LCMS that keeps 100% of learning materials and progress on one digital platform.
- Lower cost over time: 40% less time spent authoring content, since digital materials can be reused; less spent on printing and traditional facilities.

**Teaching**

- Less administrative load: lesson prep, marking and statistical reporting are automated.
- Stronger professional practice: teachers move from "delivering knowledge" to guiding the class, freeing them for creative work and deeper research.

**The learning experience**

- Adaptability: students build the habit of learning on their own and working in teams.
- Learning without limits: interactive materials (video, AR/VR, interactive quizzes) available anytime, anywhere, on a phone.
- Better results: an expected 20% gain in how well knowledge sticks, through active learning and visual interaction.

## Slide 10 — Information security and document leak prevention

- **Dynamic watermark** — Identifying details are overlaid faintly across the screen. If a page is photographed on the sly, the authorities can trace it straight away.
- **Technical barrier against copying** — Download, copy/paste and screen capture (PrintScreen) are fully disabled for internal and classified documents.
- **Access log** — Every account's access, open and read history is recorded in full, in real time.

*Meets the strict rules on copying and circulating documents set out in the 2018 Law on Protection of State Secrets and Decree 26/2020/ND-CP.*

**On screen**

- Technical barrier against copying — Download: fully locked (no download button, download API disabled) · Copy/Paste: fully blocked (Ctrl+C and right-click disabled) · PrintScreen: disabled (the screen capture key is blocked)

## Slide 15 — AI attendance system

**Tighter discipline**
Training regulations are properly enforced, and each class keeps its routine and discipline. Biometric face recognition puts a stop to people attending or signing in for someone else — whoever is in the room is the officer or learner who was actually assigned to the course.

**Time saved**
Attendance happens automatically and quickly, without interrupting the lecturer or the flow of the lesson.

**Transparent reporting**
The system stores attendance data on its own and produces clear statistical reports in real time.

## Slide 16 — AI support for research

*The AI is trained entirely in-house on the school's own data. Question-and-answer content is never sent out to public servers (no reliance on OpenAI/ChatGPT), which keeps information secure and ideologically sound.*

- **Data analysis support** — Summarises and visualises figures; processes data to whatever output is required.
- **Suggesting an approach** — Researches, analyses and proposes a suitable framework and methodology.
- **Challenging the argument** — Asks probing questions back to surface unstated assumptions, so an argument is properly stress-tested before a draft, a research paper or an oral defence goes forward.

## Slide 18 — How it is rolled out

**Step 1**
Put in a high-performance wide-area network. Set up cloud storage built to information-security standards, so resolutions and documents sit behind a wall separate from the school's internal internet.

**Step 2**
Set up the content management system (LCMS) and configure the AI models that analyse internal data. Adapt the teaching equipment as needed.

**Step 3**
Train staff and run a pilot at the school. Show people how to get the most out of the software and how to use AI when writing papers and clarifying an argument.
